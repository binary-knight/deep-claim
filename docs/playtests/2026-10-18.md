# Playtest 5: 2026-10-18

Fifth monitored test play and the first on spec 010 step 1 (the race for the
program), build `9f3a7e0`. Played on Windows from the share, using the CI
playtest package. Default balance, one AI rival (Northfield Minerals). One
game, seed 9199558957000, played to its end on day 6606 (Y19 M5), in about
half an hour by the file times on the share. Trace
`playtest/trace-20261018-020133.jsonl`, 8,355 records, 87 orders. It was
recorded on Windows and replayed on Linux at `90261e3` (no game code
changed since `9f3a7e0`): every one of the 6,607 days matched the client's
state hash. One F9 marker and screenshot, of the end screen.

The trace was watched live during play. The owner's impressions were given
after the game ended and are quoted below.

## Findings, by weight

### 1. It did not feel like a race, and it was decided by day 2406
The owner did not know there was a race until they lost. The three stage
research clocks (480, 1,680 and 2,520 days) set a floor of 4,680 days on any
founding, and Northfield started its stage 1 research on day 1138. On
research time alone, the last day the player could have started stage 2
research and still finished stage 3 by day 6606 was day 2406 (Y7 M9). The
player had about $2M then, against the $10M stage 2 research costs, with
$10M of credit unused. They started it on day 3780, 1,374 days too late.
Nothing on screen said so. "Behind" with 29 years on the deadline read as
plenty of time, and the deadline never came into play (the game ended
after 18.4 years). This is the spec's open question "a human who completes stage 1
late probably cannot catch the AI", now answered: they cannot, and they are
not told.

### 2. The player could not find nickel
Stage 2 needs 20,000 t of nickel. Nickel is found in the Northern Shield
(30% of plots, median 50,000 t), usually beside iron, and in the Copper
Belt (15%, median 17,000 t) (`data/regions.json`). The unsurveyed plot
panel lists the odds ("Nickel ore 30%"), but the owner did not take it in,
and the Program tab does not say where a needed ore comes from. The
player's 27 surveys found no nickel. Northfield delivered its 20,000 t; the
player held $375M and the iron and copper for stage 2 and could not finish
it.

The game's world, regenerated from its seed after the write-up (spec 011's
research notes), held 13 nickel plots, two of them with no iron. Northfield
bought 12; four had gone unsold at auction before it did, or before the
game ended. None of the 24 plots the player surveyed held nickel. The owner's reading, that ores might need
smelting into nickel, also points at step 2 (plants): the game will need to
show which goods come from where.

### 3. Money stops mattering by the middle of the game

| Day | Cash |

| 2520 | $0.4M |
| 3600 | $25.1M |
| 4680 | $156.0M |
| 5400 | $329.6M |
| 6606 (end) | $374.5M |

Early on, cash was the binding constraint: it held the player under the $10M
stage 1 cost until day 1830 and under the $10M for stage 2 research until
about day 3100. From about day 4000 the player could have paid stage 2's
$60M and stage 3's $150M several times over. Everything the player
researched, both program stages included, cost $34.8M in all ("super easy,
I just blew through it"). This matches the step 1 note that the AI
ends rich; the human does too, once past the squeeze.

### 4. The clock stops without saying why
The clock stopped 58 times: 20 finished surveys, 13 losing-mine notices, 12
finished research projects, 6 auctions won, 1 lost, and 6 rival program
events (the founding included). Each wrote "Paused: …" to the status line,
but at speed the owner did not see why the game had stopped, and the log
scrolled too fast to read. The rival's program events, the stops that
mattered most, looked the same as the 52 routine ones.

### 5. Mines run short, and the running-low warning is unreliable

| Plot | Ore | Opened | Warning (days left) | Operating days after the warning | End |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| #25 | iron | 229 | 630 (26) | 742 | worked out 5458 |
| #67 | copper | 316 | 1320 (8) | 2,490 and running | running |
| #68 | copper | 618 | 3060 (13) | 1,901 | worked out 4961 |
| #38 | iron | 2165 | 3840 (28) | 2,560 and running | running |
| #119 | copper | 4306 | 4740 (29) | 59 | worked out 4799 |
| #88 | lithium | 4306 | 5400 (15) | 301 | worked out 5701 |
| #73 | copper | 5926 | 6210 (28) | 143 | worked out 6353 |

The mines ran on for 2 to 300 times as long as the warning said. Plots #25,
#67, #68 and #38 were surveyed with an error of x0.4 or x0.5, which may
explain the worst cases; #119, #88 and #73 were surveyed at x0.16 and the
warning was still out by a factor of 2 to 20. Three of the nine mines lasted under 500 days (#119 cost $592k for the
plot plus the build). Late in the game the player re-surveyed their own
mines (days 5373 to 5394) before deciding which to reopen: they no longer
trusted the warning. This is evidence for step 3 (mine life).

### 6. Forced sales took the ore held for the program
With auto-sell off, the month-end cover-costs sale sold the most valuable
stock first. Once the iron was delivered (day 1147), that was the copper
being held for stage 1: 1,847 t on day 1170, 2,137 t on day 1230, 2,480 t
on day 1260. Storage fees came to $76.6M over the game, most of it on an
iron pile that reached 1.08M t against the 600,000 t stage 2 needs.

### 7. Smaller things
- The sell reserve made sense to the owner. The trace shows it set on a
  switched-off policy three times (days 315, 689, 5539), where it does
  nothing, and auto-sell switched on, off and on again on day 1172; the
  owner did not report confusion.
- The end screen's "Mines 9" counts worked-out mines. Four were running.
- A second bid on the same plot (#66, days 5734 and 5746, same amount)
  replaced the first, as the sim intends; the screen gave no sign that a
  bid was already standing. Related to the known second-Deliver-click item.
- A survey of #111 was still out when the plot was withdrawn from auction
  (day 5730), so its $150k bought a report on land that could not be bid
  on.
- Northfield bought land every month or two all game, 63 plots by the end.
  Copper fell 62% between days 1140 and 1500, and iron 69% from its peak
  (day 1710) to day 2160.

## The step 1 questions, answered

- **Does the deadline bite?** No. Northfield founded the program after 18.4
  years, 11.6 years before it.
- **Can a human who completes stage 1 late catch the AI?** No. 267 days
  late on stage 1 became 1,739 days late on stage 2 research, because cash
  held up the start and nothing showed the cost of waiting.
- **The AI ends rich and holds most of the land.** Holds most of the land:
  yes, 63 of 120 plots. Rich: so did the player.
- **A human's pace.** Stage 1 after 5.2 years, the early end of the
  arithmetic's years 5 to 7.
