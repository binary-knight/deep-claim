# Playtest 1: 2026-10-08

First monitored test play. Build `02929c5`, default balance, one AI rival
(Northfield Minerals), three games in one sitting of about an hour. The
session was recorded by the playtest trace (`playtest/trace-20261008-194037.jsonl`,
5,244 records) and watched live; numbers below come from the trace and from
`deep_claim_replay`, which matched the client's state hash on every one of
the roughly 2,800 days played. The sim is deterministic under real play.

Orders placed: 13 surveys, 10 bids, 8 builds, 11 sales, 4 loans, 4 research,
1 mothball. Two rejections, both harmless double submissions (a second
"sell all" and a second 10M loan).

## Findings, by weight

### 1. Unsold ore is the dominant way to lose

Every game hit the insolvency warning while holding ore worth several times
the shortfall: 3.7M in game 1, 10M and then 20M in game 3. Storage is 5% of
base price per tonne-month, so a pile of 60k t of iron costs 190k a month
and a pile of 20k t of copper 900k a month. Nothing in the interface relates
the warehouse to the cash line, and at 20x a month passes in 1.5 seconds.

Decision taken with the player during the session: add standing sell
policies, set per resource by one command and applied by the sim every day
in the market step so the AI can use them too. Four modes were chosen:

- sell all output daily (default for new mines);
- keep a reserve of N tonnes, sell the rest;
- sell only at or above a floor price;
- cover costs: at month end, sell just enough to keep cash above zero.

The dashboard should also show the warehouse's monthly storage cost next to
cash and a plain warning when it exceeds a share of cash.

### 2. Nothing pauses the game when it matters

The insolvency warning, a mine opening, an auction result and a survey
result all scroll past at 20x. Game 1 died five in-game months after the
first warning without the player seeing it. Add auto-pause on a configurable
set of notices (insolvency warning and bankruptcy on by default; auction
won or lost, survey complete and mine operational as options), with the
notice shown in the status line.

### 3. The locked-resource trap

Lithium is unlocked by Lithium Processing (4M, 240 days, two prerequisites).
A survey shows lithium like any other ore, the Build button says nothing,
and an operating mine with only locked ore extracts nothing while charging
full opex. The player lost 5.2M of capex plus 400 days of opex to this.

Fixes: survey results label locked resources ("needs Lithium Processing");
the build panel says "nothing extractable yet" when every known deposit is
locked; and a mine whose extractable deposits are all empty or locked
should not charge full opex. Open question for the player: should such a
mine mothball itself (opex at 15%) until something becomes extractable?

### 4. Empty plots and unsurveyed buys

Three of the thirteen surveyed plots had no deposits at all (#11, #17, #21).
That is by design (regional presence odds), but one unsurveyed purchase of
2M plus capex ends a game in the opening. Keep the design; make the bid
panel say "not surveyed" in the same place it shows the odds, and make a
mine that finds nothing on opening say "no ore found" instead of
"operational" followed by "depleted".

### 5. Auctions: the rival pays 11 to 32% over the ask

The player bid exactly the ask on both contested plots and lost both; the
uncontested ones were won at the ask. The AI's bid jitter is invisible, and
the interface does not say that a repeat bid replaces the earlier one
(it does). Show the bid count and closing day in the plot table, say
"bidding again replaces your bid", and consider a hint that contested plots
clear above the ask.

### 6. The rival expands much faster than a player can

By year 4 the rival held 11 to 16 plots against the player's 2 to 4, and
seven or more mines. Part of this is the player's stalls (200 days before
the first build in game 1), but the AI buys roughly a plot a quarter from
day 45 onwards regardless of its own cash flow signals. Moving the AI knobs
to `data/ai.json` (already planned) and tuning its purchase cadence toward
parity with a competent player is the fix; the sim_runner sweeps should
report plots per company at years 2, 5 and 10.

### 7. Opening pace

With 5M, a 150k survey, a 1 to 2M plot, 1.5 to 4M capex and a 180-day
build, the first income arrives around day 250 at the earliest and the
first positive month around day 450 in the good game. That is slow but not
wrong for the genre; the real problem was that the first mistake is fatal.
Candidates if it still feels slow after the fixes above: 120-day build or
lower capex per hectare. Not changing yet.

### 8. Markets

Copper carried the winning game: about 870 a tonne against 8k a day opex at
60 t a day. Iron is thin: base 60, down to 37 with the rival's supply, and
one 88k t dump (55 days of the whole market's demand) will crater it. The
sell policies fix the dumps. No price change proposed.

### 9. Smaller interface findings

- A queued order has no visible state while paused, so "sell all" and
  "take loan" were each double-clicked once. Disable or badge the button
  until the order is applied.
- The mine on plot #14 depleted after 470 days with no warning. Show an
  estimated days-remaining figure on operating mines (from the survey
  estimate and the current output) and a notice a month ahead.
- The finance card should show the monthly net (income less opex, storage
  and instalments) and months of runway at the current burn.
- Survey results should show an estimated value (tonnes times today's
  price) so plots can be compared at a glance.
- There is no way to sell or abandon a plot. A "list for sale" order at a
  price the owner sets, sold through the normal auction, is the natural
  addition. Deferred: it needs AI buying logic too.
- Re-surveying is allowed and re-rolls the error; an empty result is
  definitive. Say so in the survey panel.

### 10. Tooling

The trace, screenshots and replay worked as designed and the live watch
caught every problem above as it happened. Two gaps found while watching:
notice kinds in the trace are snake_case (fine, just undocumented in the
filter used), and a status line that expires leaves no record. Codex's
review of the tooling was applied in `95a243e`.

## Proposed order of work

1. Standing sell policies (sim command, view, AI use, interface).
2. Auto-pause on notices (session and interface only).
3. Locked-resource labelling, "nothing extractable" build warning, no full
   opex on a mine with nothing to extract.
4. Plot table: bid count, closing day, surveyed flag; repeat-bid note.
5. Finance card: monthly net and runway; warehouse storage cost warning.
6. Days-remaining estimate on mines; "no ore found" wording.
7. AI knobs to data and cadence tuning, with runner sweeps.
8. Save slots and confirmations (from the earlier Phase A plan).
9. Plot resale (later).
