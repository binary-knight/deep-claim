- **Opening (005):** three of four opening plots were mineable and the

  366.
- **World (005):** new land was still being listed in year 9.

## Findings, by weight

### 1. Orders placed while paused give no feedback (owner, during play)
A survey, bid or build ordered while the clock is stopped changes nothing
on screen until the game is unpaused. The trace shows the cost: on day 0
and again on day 1950 the same survey was ordered twice and the second
bounced ("a survey crew is already out on that plot"). It applies to every
order: survey, bid, build, research, loans, mine state. The button should
change at once ("Survey ordered"), the cost should show as committed, and
a paused turn should read as a list of pending decisions. Whether a
pending order can be cancelled before the clock restarts is a design
choice.

### 2. Every market is flooded for the whole game
At the end supply was five times demand in all four markets: iron 8,849
t/day against 1,600, copper 997 against 200, nickel 209 against 60,
lithium 81 against 16. Prices sat at 0.35 to 0.5 times base from year 3
on (iron $40 on a $90 base, copper $385 on $900, lithium $4.9k on $14k).
This is the target spec 006 missed, seen in play. Mines stay profitable
at those prices, so nothing stops either company building. The fix is in
the market's demand numbers, which spec 006 was not allowed to touch.

### 3. The rival is "a peer" only against itself
Spec 006 measured the rival against another AI (1.08x). Against a careful
human it bought 44 plots to the player's 4. That is not unfair, since the
player had the money to buy and did not, but it is the rival that floods
the markets in finding 2, and the player has no reason to keep up.

### 4. Money stops mattering after the first mine
One iron mine on a major deposit earned about $1M a month from year two.
Cash was never a constraint again: $62M by year 5, $177M at the end, with
33 orders in eight years and one stretch of 900 days without a land
decision. The same finding as playtest 2, a little later in the game.
Deposits now end (the rival had depleted mines by the close) but the
player's 5M t find will outlast any game.

### 5. Half of all surveys find nothing
Five of the ten surveys reported no mineable ore (#61, #86, #100, #108,
#116), four of them on Salt Flats at $150k and 20 days each. Worth a look
at whether the plot table makes the odds clear before the money is spent.

### 6. Tooling
- The trace's `build` field showed the previous merge. The build id is
  read from git when the build is configured, not when it is compiled.
- The live monitor's notice filter matched nothing this session; the
  notice kinds it greps for should be taken from the trace format.

### 7. Smaller things
- A $10M loan could be taken with $136M in the bank. Harmless, but the
  form gives no hint that it is pointless.
- With auto-sell off, lithium sat for nine months paying storage with
  only the monthly storage line to say so.
- The log is now mostly the rival: 48 "plot sold" and 48 "mine started"
  lines, nearly all Northfield's.
