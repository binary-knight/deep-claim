# ADR-F-2026-10-10-markets-that-bite-001: Running cost per ore, chosen from observed output

**Date:** 2026-10-10
**Status:** Accepted
**Context:** A mine's running cost was one figure, $8,000 a day, for every ore. At base output that put break-even at 11 to 22% of base price, so no realistic oversupply could push a price below cost and price never forced a decision (spec 008 research). The owner chose a daily cost per ore, set in data so each ore's break-even is a stated share of base price. Plots can hold more than one ore, some ores are locked behind research, and true deposits are hidden from every view.
**Decision:** Each ore in `data/resources.json` carries `mine_opex_per_day`. A mine's running cost is the highest cost among its cost ores, times the existing research and event multipliers, and times the mothball share when mothballed. A mine's cost ores are the ores with non-zero output on its most recent operating day; before it has produced anything, they are the ores its owner can extract. The single `mine_opex_per_day` in `data/balance.json` is removed, and `CompanyView::mineOpexQuote` becomes one quote per ore.
**Consequences:** Break-even can be tuned per ore and tested against a stated range. A cost shown to the player can never reveal an unsurveyed ore, because it derives from output the player already sees. A mine's cost can change when an ore runs out or a research unlock adds one, so the mine panel must show the current cost. A two-ore mine pays one cost for two revenues, which makes mixed plots worth more than before. A mine on its first day may be charged the dearest extractable ore's cost for one day. Cost that varies with depth or grade is left to mine economics (Earth strategy system 2).
